About

Learned perception, policies, and controllers now span the robotics stack, but long-standing robustness problems remain: the sim-to-real gap, compounding errors, sensor degradation, and out-of-distribution inputs. Robotic foundation models sharpen the issue. Clutter, small changes in viewpoint or lighting, and perturbed instructions or sensors can drastically reduce success, and benchmark scores do not reliably predict behavior with novel objects, in new scenes, or on diverse embodiments.

This workshop brings together researchers from perception, planning, control, and robot learning, across academia and industry, to examine why learned systems fail outside the lab and what makes them reliable. Through invited talks, paper presentations, video demos of robot successes and failures, two robot learning challenges, and a closing panel, we will compare how different communities define, measure, and mitigate failure.

This workshop asks: How robust are policies to perturbed or adversarial instructions, sensors, and actuation? What do clutter, moving objects, and uncertain contact demand? How well do policies adapt to unseen environments, objects, and embodiments? How can robots detect failures at runtime and recover safely? Which evaluation protocols truly measure real-world robustness?

Challenges

Dates and participation details will be posted here.

Challenge 1

Clutter and Perturbations

Various manipulation tasks in office, study, and kitchen scenes. Clutter increases from clean scenes to dense distractors in the robot's path, combined with controlled sensor and instruction perturbations, so failures can be attributed to perception, grounding, or planning.

Ranking: success rate, then collision rate, across clutter and perturbation levels.

Challenge 2

Dynamics

Dynamic manipulation with physically simulated moving objects and uncertain contact. Tasks probe trajectory prediction, periodic patterns, dynamic avoidance, state transitions, and self-triggered motion, with difficulty rising from single variations to combined effects.

Ranking: success rate together with a partial score that credits progress on incomplete tasks.

Data, baselines, and evaluation code are ready for release. Entries are evaluated online on held-out conditions, and winners present their results at the workshop.

Call for papers

We invite 2- to 6-page papers following ICRA'27 guidelines on robustness in perception, language grounding, planning, and control. We welcome work on manipulation, mobile and field robotics, driving, medical robotics, and other applications, as well as deployment case studies and failure reports from industry. The submissions are evaluated based on the novelty, relevance to the workshop topics, and significance.

The reviews will be double-blind. All accepted papers will be presented as posters, and the top two papers will be invited for oral presentation. The best paper and best poster will receive awards and certificates. We also invite videos of robot successes and instructive failures, which will be shown during the breaks.

Topics of interest

  • Uncertainty estimation and safety guarantees for learning-based models
  • Perturbed and adversarial sensors and instructions
  • Manipulation in clutter and unstructured environments
  • Physical reasoning and contact-rich control in dynamic scenes
  • Adaptation to unseen environments, objects, and embodiments
  • Benchmarks and evaluation protocols for robustness and safety
  • Sim-to-real transfer and real-world deployment
  • Failure detection and safe recovery
  • Reliable household and service robots in human environments
  • Long-term reliability of robots in real-world environments
